FOUNDATION · What an LLM does
Slide 02 / 17
i · The autocomplete that read the internet

It does one thing — guesses the next word.

Every sentence Claude produces is one token at a time, choosing what is most likely to come next based on everything that came before. No reasoning engine. No database lookup. Just a very, very well-read predictor of the next word.

FOUNDATION · Knowledge cutoff
Slide 04 / 17
iii · Trained once · Answers forever

It does not know today.

The model was trained on data up to a certain date — its knowledge cutoff. After that, the world kept moving but the model didn't. Today's NIFTY close, this morning's RBI rate decision, the result the company posted at 3:45 PM — none of it is inside the model. If you want it in the answer, you have to put it in the prompt.

Inside the model
2010Cutoff · Jan 2026Today
Inside
Wyckoff phases. Every doji ever written about. The 2020 covid crash. Every Murphy chapter.
Inside
NIFTY's history through late 2025. Every annual report up to FY24.
Outside
Today's open. This morning's news. The rate cut announced at 11:30 AM. The price you're staring at right now.

FOUNDATION · How you use it
Slide 05 / 17
iv · A conversation, not a search

Don't search. Talk.

Path A · Search habit

Type once. Read once. Give up.

›One-shot question, hope for gold.
›First answer doesn't fit → close tab.
›No follow-up, no refinement.
›Treats AI like a slot machine.
Path B · Conversation habit

Refine in turns. Push back. Reshape.

›"That's not what I meant — try again, this time…"
›Adds context piece by piece.
›Asks why, asks for sources, asks for the counter.
›Treats AI like a junior analyst at a desk.
The first answer is the worst answer. The fifth is usually the one worth keeping.

BEHAVIOR · Prompt = question + setup
Slide 06 / 17
v · Garbage in, confident garbage out

The prompt is the work.

Fuzzy prompt
// what most people type
Hey Claude, is this chart
looking good?
No instrument. No timeframe. No criteria. Claude will guess what you want — and answer confidently. Confidence ≠ correctness.
Precise prompt
// the prompt that earns its answer
Role: Strict Dow theorist.
Chart: NIFTY · 1-hour · last 60 bars (attached).
Task: Label the phase using Dow tenets 4 + 5.
       Cite the bar that confirms.
If unclear: say so. Don't fabricate.
Role + Context + Task + Honesty clause. Same model, same chart — completely different answer.

BEHAVIOR · Context window
Slide 07 / 17
vi · The desk it works on

Working memory, not the library.

The model's context window is everything currently on its desk — the prompt, the chart you uploaded, the prior turns of this chat. If it isn't on the desk, it isn't in the answer. The library (its training) is in another room.

Rule of thumbPaste it · upload it · or it didn't happen.
Why it matters"Last week we discussed…" — not in a new chat, it didn't.

Outside the desk: yesterday's chat · your other Claude conversation · the chart you forgot to upload · today's live price.

BEHAVIOR · Tokens — what you're billed on
Slide 08 / 17
vii · The unit of work

Words aren't free. Tokens are the meter.

i · The unit
≈1.3tokens/word

A token is roughly a word-piece.

"Trading" might be one token. "Disinflation" is split into three. English averages ~1.3 tokens per word.

ii · The bill
In + Out

You're charged on both ends.

What you send (prompt + uploads + chat history) plus what the model writes back. A long chart-analysis chat is not free — even on Claude.ai.

iii · The ceiling
200Ktypical

Each chat has a context budget.

Cross it and old turns silently drop off the desk. "It used to know that" — yes, until the context filled up.

Quick math
A 10-page PDF you upload ≈ ~5,000 tokens. Your 2-hour TA chat ≈ ~15,000 tokens. Comfortable headroom — until you keep adding charts.
~1.3 × words

BEHAVIOR · Hallucination
Slide 09 / 17
viii · Confidently wrong

When it doesn't know, it guesses — without telling you.

i

Invented citations

"Per Murphy, page 187…" — page doesn't exist. Sounds authoritative. Often isn't.

ii

Fabricated numbers

"NIFTY closed at 24,832 on Mar 14, 2024…" — could be off by 500 points. No flag.

iii

Phantom patterns

Asked "is this a head & shoulders?" — it will find one. Pattern-matching to please you.

iv

Made-up APIs

Writes Python code that calls yfinance.get_nifty_options(). That function doesn't exist. The code looks real.

Hallucination is not a bug — it's the same next-word machine doing its job when the truthful next-word isn't in reach. Verify the load-bearing claim. Always.

BEHAVIOR · Temperature & randomness
Slide 10 / 17
ix · Same question, four answers

It's not a database.

Run 01

"Accumulation — bar 41 confirms."

Confident. Cites a bar. Maps to Wyckoff phase 1.

Run 02

"Markup phase, momentum solid."

Same chart. Different label. Same model. Different roll of the dice.

Run 03

"Re-accumulation within an uptrend."

A hedge of the prior two. Sounds smart. Means almost nothing.

Run 04

"Unclear — need more context."

The honest one. Rarest. Reward this answer when it shows up.

Ask the same thing twice, get two answers. Temperature is the dial that controls how random. Useful for brainstorming. Dangerous for decisions. Always run twice on the calls that matter.

REALITY · Bad at math, good at language
Slide 11 / 17
x · Capability map

Use it for words. Use a sheet for numbers.

✓ Strong

Naming the pattern

Bull-flag · doji · failed breakout · phase. Language-shaped tasks are the home turf.

✓ Strong

Reading the story

"What's the narrative this chart is telling?" — summaries, bias-spotting in a journal, postmortems.

× Weak (raw)

Multi-step arithmetic

"P&L = (254.75 − 238.50) × 75 × 3 lots" — confident, often wrong. Decimals slip. Margin math slips.

× Weak (raw)

Percentage chains

"7.4% of ₹2.38L on a ₹14L portfolio drawn-down 12%…" — sounds computed, often guessed.

Nuance: Claude.ai and ChatGPT can now write & run Python on the fly — math becomes reliable. Rule: the model alone is bad at math; the model + calculator is fine. Always ask: is it computing, or guessing-that-looks-like-computing?

REALITY · What it can & can't see
Slide 12 / 17
xi · The blind partner at the desk

It does not see your screen.

It sees what you paste. It sees what you upload. It does not see your terminal, your TradingView tab, your Sensibull positions, the WhatsApp message from your broker. Treat AI like a partner sitting in another city on a voice call — you have to describe the chart for it to help with the chart. If it's not in the message, it's not in the model.

REALITY · Multimodal in 2026
Slide 13 / 17
xii · Three ways to feed it

It reads more than text now.

i

The chart

Screenshot from TradingView. Drag, drop, ask. "Label the Dow phase on this NIFTY hourly." It can see the candles.

format · PNG · JPG
ii

The PDF

Annual report. Result PDF. Broker margin policy doc. Drop it in, ask "what changed in capex guidance year-over-year."

format · PDF up to ~32MB
iii

The CSV

Your trade journal. Your option-chain dump. "Find every trade where I exited within 5 minutes of a 1% drawdown." Pattern-spotting at scale.

format · CSV · XLSX · TXT

In 2026, "AI for traders" is not a text-only conversation. The chart, the journal, the document — all go in the same chat.

REALITY · Memory across chats
Slide 16 / 17
xv · A fresh page, every time

Each new chat forgets the last one.

By default, Claude doesn't remember yesterday's conversation. Every new chat is a new desk, new intern, no notes from before. Projects change this — you give the AI a folder of your trading rules, your journal, your watchlist, and every chat inside that Project starts already aware. One folder. Many fresh conversations. That's how a trader builds an AI that knows them.
